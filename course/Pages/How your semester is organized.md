Students’ Overview – Complex Software Systems This page provides an explanation of the Complex Software Systems semester (30 EC). Its purpose is to give a quick insight into the structure of the semester, the educational vision, the division of roles, and the key moments for alignment, supervision, and assessment. 

## Purpose and Educational Vision

The  *Complex Software Systems* semester is designed to have students work on authentic, complex software challenges in collaboration with external stakeholders (clients such as Partners in Innovation and research groups). 

Core principles: 

- Engineering mindset: working in progressive steps with short feedback cycles
- Systems thinking: attention to the coherence between architecture, data, processes, and stakeholders 
- Quality & maintainability as a structural part of design and implementation 
- Room for individual learning paths through individual experiments (personal flavours) 

What we want you to learn and demonstrate is how you: 

- professionally approach complex software problems 
- collaborate effectively within a team and with external stakeholders 
- make well-considered choices regarding architecture, quality, and maintainability 
- demonstrate areas in which you want to specialize further (your personal flavour) 
- critically reflect on your own growth and decisions 

## Semester Structure 

The semester consists of two parallel learning activities: 

- Group Project (GP) – the main time investment 
- Individual Experiments (IE / Personal Flavours) – deepening or broadening 

For the group project, you will work in a team of 4–6 students on an authentic assignment from an external stakeholder. There you: apply an Agile way of working, focus on both functional and non-functional requirements and on a combination of implementation, research, and advisory work. 

For the individual experiment, you will deepen your core software engineering skills and make individual competencies visible. It is also a way to further personalize the educational program and align it with your personal preferences, for example through experimentation via rapid prototyping in a domain of your choice (e.g. cloud-native, data-driven, cybersecurity, agentic AI). 

It is important that you make your choices consciously and can explain them in your portfolio. Therefore, substantiate what you do and make clear what it delivers. Discuss this with your lecturers, fellow students, and the client. In this way, you develop the knowledge, skills, and professional attitude as described in the learning outcomes and apply them in practice. Workshops, community activities, and fixed feedback moments support you in this development. From lecturers you can expect that they: 

- help formulate research questions 
- stimulate collaboration (within the group, with the client, and with other project teams) 
- challenge architectural and quality-related choices 
- validate intermediate products for the portfolio 
- provide feedback on process and reflection (not only on the end result), covering both technical and professional (learning) skills 

The semester is divided into sprints of approximately three weeks. The exact planning is aligned by each project group with the client and coaches.  3. Planning Important moments during this semester:

## Assessment 

Assessment is holistic and longitudinal. Important principles include: 

- Everything counts: products, process, professionalism, and development 
- Feedback is leading; assessment follows from this 
- Students actively collect feedback themselves (feedback triangulation) 
- Development-oriented feedback scale: undefined, orienting, beginning, proficient, advanced 
- Minimum rule: if all learning outcomes are demonstrated at least at proficient level, the result is satisfactory or higher. All other assessments are discussed in the assessor meeting, where the final joint assessment takes place.

> **Additional explanation: Students actively collect feedback themselves (feedback triangulation)**
> Students are used to asking feedback from their regular coach(es) in previous semesters. In this semester, students are expected to **actively seek relevant, additional feedback**. 
> Each student follows a different learning path, so they must consciously consider which expertise adds value. This (self-regulating) skill is taken into account in both supervision and assessment.

## Personal Learning Plan (PLP) and Portfolio 

Important instruments for assessment are the Personal Learning Plan (PLP), which is part of the assessment portfolio. 

The PLP helps you shape your learning process in a goal-oriented way. You translate learning outcomes into concrete learning goals and actions, take ownership of your learning, and describe what you will actually do. Use the PLP to engage in dialogue with lecturers and to build your portfolio. The PLP is a living development plan: you create it at the start of the semester and continuously adjust it. 

Throughout the semester, you develop various professional products, request feedback on them, and have final products validated by a lecturer. These are then collected in a portfolio, together with reflections and received feedback.

## Practical Agreements 

- Learning is not done alone: share what you are doing with your group members. Exchange with other groups and classes so you know what others are working on. Show lecturers your progress and proactively involve others in your learning. 
- Communication primarily takes place via the LMS environment 
- Lecturer teams regularly align through intervision and calibration 
- Milestones are coordinated and communicated well in advance with students (e.g. documented in Canvas) 
- Shared responsibility for consistency and quality of supervision
