Below for each learning outcome outcome based criteria are specified that guide you in discovering what kind of work could be considered relevant for your learning outcomes.

These rubrics are used as an instrument to assess your work during portfolio discussion, but can also be used as an discussion instrument with your teachers. For each rubric you can question yourself: How did I achieve this part? Did I achieve this part? In which deliverables do I demonstrate this part? Etcetera. 

We expect that you do not demonstrate behaviour once, but that you clearly demonstrate how this behaviour is realised in different projects or products.

The rubrics are defined as _single point rubrics_. It describes intended behaviour by the end of the semester. In each portfolio review teachers can indicate where you have demonstrated work and where there is still work to do. Feedback can also be that you have exceeded expectations.

## **  Software Quality Control  
**

You demonstrate **sustainable software quality** in all your work, by systematically addressing both functional and non-functional requirements of a software system, and **continuously validating** system behavior against **well-defined quality** benchmarks.  

### Single-Point Rubric: Software Quality Control

|**Areas for Growth (Below Standard)**|**Core Outcome Standard (Proficient)**|**Evidence of Excellence (Above Standard)**|
|---|---|---|
|**1. Sustainable Software Quality**<br><br>  <br>  <br>  <br><br>_[Treats quality as a short-term fix; fails to adapt to modern practices like AI-specific needs or UX; neglects software change skills or allows code health to degrade unchecked.]_|**Sustainable Software Quality**<br><br>  <br><br>You apply practices that support the long-term sustainability of software while adapting to the newest industry practices (e.g., AI-specific requirements, UX). You actively aim at sharpening your software change skills, maintaining structural code health by applying previous semester knowledge of metrics and refactoring practices.|_[Demonstrates exceptional foresight in software evolution, proactively designing code health safeguards that effortlessly absorb future changes without accumulating technical debt.]_|
|**2. Continuous Validation**<br><br>  <br>  <br>  <br><br>_[Relies on ad-hoc or manual testing; setup lacks reproducibility or diagnostics; fails to use validation feedback from the development environment to guide improvements.]_|**Continuous Validation**<br><br>  <br><br>You apply best practices to measure system qualities over time in a setup that is effective in reproducibility and diagnostics. You build upon prior knowledge from earlier semesters to realise a development environment with automated pipelines, using validation feedback to drive system improvements and underpinned decisions.|_[Engineers an advanced, automated validation pipeline with elite diagnostic capabilities that autonomously monitors system behavior and triggers continuous optimization.]_|
|**3. ## Personal Leadership

**Goals and actions** 

- You proactively set goals to match your growth aspirations. 
    
- You take actions that contribute to achieving your goals. 
    
- You facilitate learning within the others (eg. stakeholders, fellow developers). 
    
- You actively search for feedback, reflect and act upon it to improve your skills. 
    
- You validate whether you are getting closer to these goals and adjust, if necessary. 
    

**Long-term development**  

- You are aware of developments in the field of software engineering and the multiple career paths.  
    
- You determine what kind of ICT professional you want to be and adjust your personal development plans, if necessary.  
    
- Based on this, you choose and prepare for your future.
    

Idea: Create for the group projects a list with questions that map to the learning outcome and challenge to make things concrete. For example: 

Engineering: What is the environment the product has to run in at the customer? How would it integrate? What are restrictions we have? 

Quality: What are our quality drivers? Which tools did we use for them? Are those industry or company accepted? How can we measure or qualify our quality criteria? Do we act towards our quality criteria (stop a release, when it is not met).? How do we evaluate quality in the different phases of our project (developemtn, Test, Acceptance, Operation)
