## What


> You demonstrate the **sustainable software quality** by systematically addressing both functional and non-functional requirements of a software system, and **continuously validating** system behavior against **well-defined quality** benchmarks.   

In this learning outcome software quality plays an important role. Activities you undertake should aim at training you as an engineer to make sure that quality is and can be met within the specific context of your project and the resources you have to your availability (for example: existing systems, money, people, expertise, etcetera). How can you define measurable quality criteria of your software system functionals and non-functionals by design? Secondly, how do you assure that you maintain a good view on if these criteria stay within their boundaries during the full lifecycle of the software system? 

### Why

In quality frameworks like [ISO-25010](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_25010)  there are over 30 quality criteria defined that can be used as criteria to develop an architecture, implement a software system, and be continuously monitored in an automated DevOps environment. However, in practice you have limited resources or other restrictions that make it impossible to focus on all these quality criteria at the same time. Or just because quality criteria conflict with each other. For example: Achieving both highest security and highest performance possible might be quite a challenge. 

## How

Quality is perceived through both functional and non-functional requirements of a software system.  For this reason, you need to aim your analysis, design and implementation at both.  

In the Canvas course we provide you with a few starting points to start the exploration and experience the process of working towards a software solution that meets the quality expectations of the customer. 

The concepts presented in the pages below should be studied and determined which specific quality criteria fit with the projects you are working on.
