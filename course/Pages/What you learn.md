By the end of this semester you will have learned what is needed to create software solutions in complex software environments. You will learn that complexity can have many faces. You will discover and explore new technologies and methodologies that help you in managing this complexity.

You will learn how software engineering, or engineering in general, is a mindset and way of working that controls complexity and uncertainty. 

You will learn how complexity can be controlled by making all your requirements measurable and steer on the practical measured outcomes of your products. You will see how continuous feedback can help you in making underpinned decisions. You will have to investigate which tools and methods fit in the context of your specific environments, that make it possible to continuously gather and steer on measured feedback.

You will also learn how by having measurable quality criteria you can make underpinned decisions for your architecture choices. Which architectural patterns could be relevant for example performance? what are the trade-offs to take into account? Which one fits best. You are not only making selections based on theory, but have quick feedback by fast prototyping possible solutions to discover the solution might be feasible. 

Might be feasible is important! There is always uncertainty that solutions work like expected. In the lab or the development machine it might seem the perfect solution, in reality it might prove different. How can you get this information if it works in the field and get even noticed on unforeseen situations? You will learn how you can design and implement a fitting operational environment in which you can collect & monitor important information at runtime, that helps you to react pro-actively and provides the information for troubleshooting & analysis. Not only technically, but also of the development environment & processes.

The semester is also a preparation towards the graduation semester. You will work as a group on the project in a way that resembles a graduation project. It might seem that you customer knows what he or she wants, but do they really? Do they oversee the implications? In practice it is easy to come with a set of functional requirements, but in practice you have to take into account more and make choices. Maybe even advice the customer to do things otherwise than suggested and reason why.
