## **What** 

Welcome to the world of complex software systems!  
  
As third-year software engineering students, you are moving towards being able to build and extend systems where unpredictable interactions, scale, and human factors collide. To understand what is happening, take the time to understand what _complexity_ is.

Fred Brooks’ described in his classic paper, _[No Silver Bullet – Essence and Accident in Software EngineeringLinks to an external site.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Silver_Bullet) (1986)_ two types of complexity:

- Essential Complexity  
    _the inherent difficulty of the problem domain itself, such as modelling a unique business workflow_
- Incidental Complexity  
    _Complexity that arises from the tools, frameworks, and technologies we choose to implement the solution._

Many of the solutions he proposes in his original article are the ones we covered in this semester's learning outcomes.

## **Why** 

Complexity and _uncertainty_, are an unavoidable reality in any modern software ecosystem. You cannot fully predict how users will behave, how cloud infrastructure will scale under unexpected loads, or how business requirements will shift next quarter. Because the future of your systems cannot be fully predicted, no single curriculum can teach you every tool you will ever need in the field. Instead, your success depends on your ability to actively investigate, experiment, and adapt. This behaviour is what you should train this semester. The result of your investigations should be that you find tools that fit your specific project. 

## **  
How**

To guide your research and project work this semester, we anchor our approach in three core pillars designed to help you harness feedback loops and navigate the unavoidable uncertainty and complexity:

- **Software Engineering Mindset:**  
    _Focuses on fast prototyping, discover fitting solutions for your specific context, and establishing tight feedback loops to validate ideas early. This is all done with keeping the customer wishes in mind, but also keep a critical eye and advice your customers on better fitting solutions they did not ask for in the first place._
    
- **Software Quality Control:**  
    _Moves beyond basic functional suitability by defining explicit, measurable (non)-functional requirements that drive your major architectural decisions and select the fitting architectural patterns._
    
- **Software Delivery & Operations:**  
    Equips you with the tooling and methodologies needed to operationalize, monitor, and analyze both expected and unexpected system behavior in production environment, closing the feedback loop for your software, development processes, and organisation.
    

Your mission this semester is to use these three anchors as starting points to explore, evaluate, and master new tools and methodologies tailored to your specific project goals. So not a one specific tool, fits all approach!  
  
What specific tools or methodologies under these anchors are you most eager as a team to investigate in the first sprint for your upcoming project?  
  
**ALERT**: The pitfall of your customer software projects is that you only focus on the functional wishes of your customer. As a team you deliver the functionals and say you are done. This is too shallow and not the behavior we are looking for! Be critical, evaluate the non-functionals, really understand the underlying problems, and advice your customer on better approaches or other oppurtunities. A certainty of a software system is that it is never done.
