## **Why** 

In complex and uncertain environments, you cannot predict user needs or technical bottlenecks. Rapid prototyping is a fast mechanism to collect feedback on the feasibility of your ideas. Its primary advantages include:

- Early Risk Mitigation  
    _Uncovers architectural flaws, utility roadblocks, and misunderstood requirements before heavy capital is invested._
- Accelerated Feedback Loops  
    _Puts tangible artifacts in front of stakeholders and users immediately, replacing abstract discussions with concrete reactions._
- Flexibility  
    _Empowers teams to make quick, underpinned decisions on what the prototype reveals about the real-world context.  
      
    _

## **What** 

Rapid prototyping is a software engineering practice focused on quickly building a working, scaled-down version of a system or feature to test concepts, gather user feedback, and validate assumptions. Rather than spending months on exhaustive upfront planning, the goal is to materialize an idea fast.

Other prototype methods exist like:

- throwaway prototyping  
    _building a quick model solely to learn requirements, which is then discarded_
- evolutionary prototyping  
    _where the prototype serves as the foundation and evolves into the final product_

In this semester we explicitly focus on rapid prototyping  

## **How**

A rapid prototype process follows these steps:

- Define your hypothesis  
    _What is the experiment you want to set-up, why are you doing it, and which problem should it solve. It is also good to have an initial idea on how you could measure the outcomes and determine if the results satisfies your expectations._
- Sketch a minimum scenario that drives your fast prototype experiment
- Building just enough functionality—using low-code tools, mock APIs, or skeleton codebase
- test that hypothesis.

Today, Generative AI (GenAI) acts as a massive accelerator for this process. Instead of coding boilerplate components or writing mock data from scratch, you can use GenAI tools to instantly generate UI wireframe code, scaffold database schemas, write synthetic test data, or simulate user interactions. 

We encourage you to use these tools to supercharge your ability to iterate, learn, and make decisions or formulate advice.
