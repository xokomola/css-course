We allow and stimulate the use of generative AI in your study [(Fontys ICT GenAI Toolbox)](https://fhict.instructure.com/courses/14338), however we also want you to be transparent on the use of GenAI and set some expectations. This page describes practical guidelines and expactations when you are using GenAI in your study. These are based on the general guidelines in the [Fontys ICT AI Manifesto](https://beleidswiki.fhict.nl/doku.php?id=en:beleid:ai_manifest)  

These guidelines are based on the four core principles of the AI Manifesto: 

- **Critical thinking** - Keep evaluating, thinking of and verifying infromation that is generated. Do not take output for granted. 
- **AI as a learning partner** - You can learn with AI as a learning partner, it should not be the schoolmate who does all your work. 
- **Transparency** - Be open when you use GenAI in your learning proces, give insight how you came to your results. 
- **Ethical awareness** - Be aware of ethical implications (in general). 

## Responsible Use Principles 

### Enhance, Don't Replace 

- Use AI to amplify your capabilities, not to substitute for fundamental understanding and creativity. 
- Maintain ownership of your learning and development journey. Make it clear why you decide to learn specific topics and how AI helped you in this proces. 
- Develop skills that complement AI capabilities, focus on the fundamentals of the semester to develop a deeper understanding on: software proces, software qualitygile methodologies. 

### Professional Standards 

- Recognize that by using AI you have the chance to prepare for professional environments where AI literacy and agile practices are essential. 
- Understand that responsible AI use involves transparency, documentation, and continuous learning and improvement. 
- Develop the ability to critically evaluate AI-generated content and integrate it meaningfully into your software projects, ensuring alignment with user needs and business goals. 

## Practical Guidelines 

### Documentation and Transparency 

- Save relevant AI sessions in your learning proces. 

 Your interactions with AI tools are part of your development and learning journey and must be available for review (3). Prompts can be discussed with your teacher. 

- AI sessions can be part of the evidence of your learning outcomes. 

### Learning Reflection and Synthesis 

Given the iterative nature of agile development and AI-assisted learning, you must: 

- Provide insight on how you refined your prompts for learning in an iterative manner 
- Summarize and reflect on your learning and development experiences where you have been using AI in your learning proces. 
- When using AI for reflection or problem-solving tasks, employ detailed prompts that demonstrate your commitment to genuine understanding and your ability to integrate AI suggestions meaningfully. 

### Coach and Stakeholder Interactions 

Be prepared that during discussions we challenge your understanding of the different topics, by asking for: 

- The reasoning behind your design and technical choices. 
- How individual software components contribute to your overall solution and meet user needs. 
- The principles and methods underlying your approach. 
- The meaning of important concepts in the semester and basic reasoning about them. 
- Your development process and insights gained through AI collaboration. 

Remember: Our goal is to prepare you as future software engineering professionals who can effectively collaborate with AI while maintaining the critical thinking, problem-solving, and domain expertise that makes you invaluable in your field. 

### Tips

- Everything starts with good and proper prompting. Take a look at this link for best practices: [https://www.promptingguide.ai/techniques/prompt_chaining](https://www.promptingguide.ai/techniques/prompt_chaining) 
- Especially in the prototyping phase, tools like [Cursor](http://cursor.com/)  and [Bolt.diy](https://github.com/stackblitz-labs/bolt.diy) can help you to quickly built proof-of-concepts with a userfriendly front-end and including concepts you find important. In the end you should of course understand what the outcomes are and how you came to the end result. In the Hackaton week, this can be also a perfect approach.
