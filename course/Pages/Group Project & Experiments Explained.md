There are two type of project this semester that you will be working on:

*   Group project
*   Personal Experiments

By working on the _group project_ and _personal experiments_, you will be gaining experience in multiple contexts and create practical results. You gather proof of your skills by collecting feedback on your contributions from your teachers, peers, and other external experts.

Group Project
-------------

This project is comparable to the group projects you know from previous semesters.  

In this course, you will work for an actual client on an authentic project comparable to the complexity of a final graduation assignment. It is the perfect environment to collaboratively train your problem-solving skills in a near-professional setting.  

For 17 weeks, your group will work during scheduled hours using a 3-week sprint methodology, concluding your work at _Innovations Insight_. 

The semester begins with a one-week exploration of your project and its domain. Based on these insights, you will formulate, together with your stakeholders, a main research question that will be the central focus for reflection during the semester.  

At the end of every sprint, you will present your latest products and insights to both your client and the teaching team. A key part of this process is the continuous investigation of _core software engineering skills_. In the first three sprints, your team will research these skills and translate them into the specific context of your project.  

After each sprint, you will evaluate your findings and decide how these new insights should fit into your project. You will then discuss these outcomes with the teaching team to determine if specific tasks need to be added to your backlog for future integration. By the end of the third sprint, you will have a  foundation and a clear plan for applying these core skills, allowing your team to spend the remaining sprints performing a technical deep dive into the project’s specific requirements or working towards for example a solid MVP.

  
Personal Experiments
-----------------------

In these projects, you are challenged to rapidly prototype emerging tools and technologies within a domain of your choice. Using a structured research approach—such as the **DOT framework**—you will design experimental setups that are thoroughly analyzed, fast prototyped, and validated. These experiments are your opportunity to prove you can independently research new technologies and apply them to test specific hypotheses.  

To ensure a steady pace of discovery, we recommend that individual experiments span no longer than two sprints. This allows you to run multiple experiments throughout the semester. While you will primarily work on these during your personal study days, it is important to schedule time during school hours to gather feedback from your teachers. We also encourage you to look beyond your immediate teaching team to find experts who match the specific technical needs of your research.  

While you have a great deal of creative freedom, your experiments must align with specific learning outcomes. You will need to demonstrate an engineering mindset, define appropriate software quality levels, and show how your findings impact long-term maintenance. Take the *core software engineering skills*  you’ve researched with your group in mind as your foundation; these individual experiments are your chance to take a deeper dive into the specialized topics that fascinate you most.
