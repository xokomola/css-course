There are two type of project this semester that you will be working on:

*   Group project (proftaak)
*   Personal Experiments

By working on the group project and personal experiments you will be gaining experience in multiple contexts for all your learning outcomes.

Group Project
-------------

This project is comparable to the group projects you know from the previous semesters.

In this course, you will work for an actual client on an authentic project designed to mirror the complexity of a final graduation assignment. It is the perfect environment to train collaboratively your problem-solving skills in a near professional setting.

For 17 weeks, your group will work during scheduled hours using a 3-week sprint methodology. At the end of every sprint, you will present your latest products and insights to both your client and the teaching team. A key part of this process is the continuous investigation of **_core software engineering skills_**. In each of the first three sprints, your team will research these skills and translate them into the specific context of your project.

After each sprint, you will evaluate your findings and decide how these new insights should fit into your project. You will then discuss these outcomes with the teaching team to determine if specific tasks need to be added to your backlog for future integration. By the end of the third sprint, you will have a solid foundation and a clear plan for applying these core skills, allowing your team to spend the remaining sprints performing a technical deep-dive into the project’s specific requirements.

  
Personal Experiments
-----------------------

In these projects, you are challenged to rapidly prototype emerging tools and technologies within a domain of your choice. Using a structured research approach—such as the **DOT framework**—you will design experimental setups that are thoroughly analyzed, executed, and validated. These experiments are your opportunity to prove you can independently research new technologies and apply them to test specific hypotheses.

To ensure a steady pace of discovery, we recommend that individual experiments span no longer than two sprints. This allows you to run multiple experiments throughout the semester. While you will primarily work on these during your personal study days, it is important to schedule time during school hours to gather feedback from your teachers. We also encourage you to look beyond your immediate teaching team to find experts who match the specific technical needs of your research.

While you have a great deal of creative freedom, your experiments must align with specific learning outcomes. You will need to demonstrate an engineering mindset, define appropriate software quality levels, and show how your findings impact long-term maintenance. Think of the core software engineering skills you’ve researched with your group as your foundation; these individual experiments are your chance to take a deeper dive into the specialized topics that fascinate you most.
