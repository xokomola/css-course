Community building is a key learning goal this semester and a core part of how students learn. The ideas behind this that relatedness helps students to feel connected to others, which strengthens intrinsic motivation and engagement in learning.  Also, through collaborative learning students can share and multiply what they learn. During this semester, we are going to motivate students to learn in both formal (planned, intentional) and informal (spontaneous, self-directed) ways.  Your role as a teacher is to help create an environment where students feel connected to others, which boosts motivation, engagement, and active participation. Students collaborate in many ways: within their own project teams, across teams, with other semesters, and with external stakeholders like teachers and clients - mirroring professional practice where interdisciplinary teamwork, feedback, and knowledge sharing are essential. 

Community is embedded in structured activities such as Software Design Meetings, Personal Experiment Meetings, poster presentations, and Innovation Insights, providing opportunities for cross-team learning, reflection, and knowledge exchange.   As a teacher, you help students navigate these interactions by connecting people and projects, making collaboration visible and meaningful, and modeling openness, approachability, and curiosity. This means actively encouraging collaboration, supporting reflection and self-regulation, and modeling the behavior you want to see so students feel safe to experiment and learn from each other. Help students to actively collaborate, seek and share feedback, and reflect on their learning while taking responsibility for their progress.

- **Facilitate collaboration** 
    - Encourage students to work in teams and across teams. 
    - Pair students for joint tasks or peer-learning activities. 
- **Connect students and projects** 
    - Link students with other groups, semesters, or external stakeholders. 
    - Introduce relevant contacts, experts, or project partners. 
- **Lead by example** 
    - Participate in discussions, workshops, and community events. 
    - Demonstrate openness, curiosity, and constructive feedback. 
- **Organize and participate in community events** 
    - Kick-off sessions to explain goals and roles. 
    - Demo days to showcase progress and outcomes. 
    - Knowledge-sharing sessions and poster presentations. 
    - Cross-team check-ins and reflection moments. 
- **Support reflection and self-regulation** 
    - Guide students in reviewing their learning goals and study plans. 
    - Ask questions that prompt students to reflect on collaboration and learning. 
- **Maintain psychological safety**
    - Create an environment where students feel safe to experiment, fail, and ask questions.  
    - Celebrate learning from mistakes, e.g., through “bug of the week” or “feature funeral” moments. 
- **Coordinate with other teachers**  
    - Align activities, expectations, and assessment. 
    - Ensure continuity and coherence across the semester. 
- **Communicate the value of community** 
    - Regularly remind students why collaboration, reflection, and knowledge sharing matter. 
    - Highlight examples of successful teamwork and peer learning.
