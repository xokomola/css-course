Below, the expectations are defined for each of the learning outcomes. This should help you to make the learning outcomes more transparent, and make it clear what the expected level is. These criteria are indicators for Proficient level, but should not be seen as a checklist. By help of discussion with your peers and teachers, you should be able to define your own activities to reach this level. 

Tip: Make it a habit to re-evaluate the learning outcomes with your team and teachers at regular intervals. For example, at the start of each new sprint. After reading the learning outcome, you can ask yourself questions like: 

- What do I understand now better? What is still not clear, and should I need to investigate further? 
- What did I do to contribute to this specific learning outcome? 
- Where are still open gaps in my work? Did I get feed-back on all my work? 

With this insight, create a list of activities for the next sprint to make a step further.

## Engineering Approach 

> You apply an **engineering approach** to create software systems in **progressive steps**, while applying **system thinking** during **informed decision making**.  

### Further clarification 

**Engineering approach** 

- You demonstrate measurable feedback cycles during your work, to validate and guide your decisions and technical solutions. 
- You apply an engineering approach to aim at improving your software engineering skills. 

**Progressive steps** 

- You demonstrate progress in taking small steps towards your goal. This could be done e.g. by evidence-based experiments with concepts relevant to your project, including the use of GenAI-assisted prototyping and validation, and decide, based on outcomes, whether an experiment might add value to the project. 

**System thinking** 

- You analyze interdependencies between components, processes, data and stakeholders, focusing on architectural connections such as data flows, communication patterns, system boundaries, and the impact of data complexity on design trade-offs.   
    
**Informed decision making**  

- You motivate your design choices based on the business context, system landscape, and project requirements, ensuring your environment is fit-for-purpose. 

## Software Quality 

> You demonstrate the **sustainable software quality** by systematically addressing both functional and non-functional requirements of a software system, and **continuously validating** system behavior against **well-defined quality** benchmarks.  

### Further clarification 

**Sustainable software quality**  

- You apply practices that support long-term sustainability of software, while adapting to the newest industry practices (eg. In fields of AI specific requirements, UX).   
- You aim at sharpening your software change skills 

**Continuous validation**  

- You apply best practices to measure system qualities over time in a setup that is effective in reproducibility, diagnostics, and supports quality improvement cycles.  

**Well-defined quality**  

- You use quality frameworks (e.g. ISO/IEC 25010 inspired) to identify and translate quality attributes such as scalability, maintainability, performance and security into software products of measurable quality. 

## Software Maintenance 

 > You apply a **maintainable** software delivery process, which is compatible with the current stakeholder's IT organization, and continuously aim for **improving the software development process** itself by applying best practices. 

### Further clarification 

**Maintainable** 

- You design, use and improve an environment and processes that support continuous integration and an ‘automate all’ approach. 
- You deploy your software in a running environment and continuously monitor the desired behavior of the system and its integrations. 
- Your environment supports team collaboration.  
- You apply a process which manages system changes. 

**Development process improvement** 

- You apply best practices to improve the development process within a team. 
-  You verify the non-functional requirements of your infrastructure  
- You advise on potential improvements of stakeholder infrastructure (?) 
- You demonstrate the feasibility to deploy your system at stakeholder organization 
- You integrate practices (e.g., DORA metrics) that improve efficiency and reliability of software delivery. 

## Professional Standard 

> You **take responsibility** when solving ICT issues. You define and carry out your **applied research** using relevant selected methodologies and provide advice to your stakeholders in **complex and uncertain contexts**. You substantiate and validate future-oriented choices by use of **law, ethical, intercultural, and sustainable arguments**. 

### Further clarification 

**Take responsibility**  

- You identify, coordinate and optimise subtasks within your projects.  
- You are transparent about the impact of the solutions you create.  
- You ensure your work is effectively transferred to the stakeholders. 

**Applied research**

- You formulate appropriate subjects to investigate within the context and can prioritise them. 
- You justify your choices of research patterns, strategies and methods used. 
- You demonstrate the reliability, validity and relevance of your research. 
- You reflect critically on the followed research process. 

**Complex and uncertain contexts** 

- You learn the domain of the project, speak the language of the domain to communicate with stakeholders. 
- You differentiate between essential and accidental complexity and take actions to understand the essential complexity in the context. 
- You stay updated on technology trends by contextualising it and share your knowledge in a meaningful way 

**Law, ethical, intercultural and sustainable** 

- You recognise and respect ethical boundaries — your own and those of others — and act with integrity and accountability. 
- You apply ethical standards and norms in the field of expertise, e.g. the GDPR or the AI Act. 
- You independently perform an impact and stakeholder analysis and provide your client with sound ethical and sustainable advice. 
- You collaborate in diverse and intercultural teams. You effectively manage cultural differences in your interactions with stakeholders. 

## Personal Leadership  

> You independently formulate **goals and actions** that demonstrate leadership in your own **long-term development** as an ICT professional. You show that you have a professional attitude and can carry out these actions and achieve your goals, adjusting them as necessary. 

### Further clarification 

**Goals and actions** 

- You proactively set goals to match your growth aspirations. 
- You take actions that contribute to achieving your goals. 
- You facilitate learning within the others (eg. stakeholders, fellow developers). 
- You actively search for feedback, reflect and act upon it to improve your skills. 
- You validate whether you are getting closer to these goals and adjust, if necessary. 

**Long-term development**  

- You are aware of developments in the field of software engineering and the multiple career paths.  
- You determine what kind of ICT professional you want to be and adjust your personal development plans, if necessary.  
- Based on this, you choose and prepare for your future.****
