During the semester you maintain a *personal development plan*. For everyone this _starts with a study plan_. These planning tools helps you and your coaches guide you through this semester. It also forms the basis under coaching meetings. 

It is short, concise and flexible: it's a tool and not a fixed formal document. We expect you to adapt it regularly and at least every sprint. It is your personal "sprint" planning. 

You review and update regularly (at least every sprint). 

You can update it using the following questions per project or experiment. You do not have to specify everything from the start but develop it throughout the semester.

## Begin with creating the study plan

Describe how you will work purposefully toward achieving the learning outcomes this semester. Translate the learning outcomes into concrete learning goals, activities, and actions, based on your starting situation, personal ambitions, and project context. Indicate your approach, planned activities and products, how you will gather feedback, and how you will monitor and adjust your progress. The study plan is a living document that you create at the start of the semester and continuously update; it forms the basis of the rest of your personal development plan (portfolio).

## Questions

### What are you going to do?

- Give a short description of the activity, product or experiment
- Link it to the Learning Outcomes that are relevant

### Why is this relevant for you?

- Describe how these activities help you demonstratie the chose learning outcomes
- Explain how it is related to the group challenge (or personal interest)

### How are you going to do it?

- Describe the main steps/approach and the means needed

### What expertise do you need?

- How and where do you gather the expertise/support needed to close the knowledge gap

## Update every sprint

- Check if the plan is still relevant for you
- Adapt: extend, change, update what is needed
- Actively use the plan in your coaching meetings
- Final reflection: describe how the plan helped you establish a path in this semester

## Guidelines

- About 1 A4
- Short and concise
- Guiding not dogmatic
- Useful for coaching meetings
- Helpful when making decisions and planning your next sprint

## Example

### Group Challenge – Smart City Mobility

The municipality of Eindhoven wants to better understand traffic flows and involve citizens more in mobility solutions. The project focuses on an interactive dashboard that supports both policymakers (organizational processes) and citizens (user interaction).

#### What are you going to do?

I will design and create a user-friendly interface in which citizens can easily view traffic congestion in their neighborhood. → Competencies: Analyzing, Designing, Realizing, Advising, Managing & Control (level 2).

#### Why is this relevant for you?

- Analyzing: by interviewing 5–6 citizens I will demonstrate that I can gather, select, and interpret user needs.
- Designing: through wireframes and a prototype I will translate insights into a visual and interactive concept.
- Realizing: a clickable prototype in Figma will make the concept testable.
- Advising: I will incorporate feedback from citizens and experts into improvements.
- Managing & Control: I will create a test plan and schedule/monitor the interviews.

#### How are you going to do it?

- Interviews with 5–6 citizens
- Wireframes and a prototype in Figma.
- Collecting feedback from a UX expert and improving the design iteratively.
- Creating and executing a test plan.

### What expertise do you need?

- A UX/UI expert for feedback on the design.
- Expertise in setting up a solid test plan.
