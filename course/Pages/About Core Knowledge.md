**What**

Core knowledge on software engineering introduces you into a set of selected concepts that can be seen as essential in the professional field when developing software in complex environments. This set will certainly be not complete and can be debated, but we think these concepts will be relevant in many of the projects you will be working on as a junior software engineer.

The concepts are also selected to offer basic knowledge which is valuable to as an orientation of the meaning of your learning outcomes. Within the *personal flavor* projects you should demonstrate basic understanding of these concepts and be able to relate them to the domains you are interested in. 

**Why**
Your credibility as a junior professional increases if you have a common vocabulary and understanding of concepts that are shared by your colleagues in the software engineering business.

In earlier semesters you already have build up some vocabulary and understanding, but for complex software systems more is needed.

We believe that basic understanding of the selected concepts and demonstration of them will help you in further exploring the software domain. 

**How**
We expect that by the end of the semester everyone has basic understanding of the concepts that are presented in the module *core knowledge*. You should be able to have a basic discussion with your peers on these concepts and you have some demonstration of applying them in practice. This can be either in individual and group work, as long as they are clear personal contributions.

Suggested approach:
- As a group pick up each sprint at least 2 new concepts to study as a group.
- Investigate the theoretical base (library strategy). Discuss and share knowledge within your team and with your teachers.
- Investigate how these new learned concepts might be relevant in the context of the group project. Discuss with your fellow students and teachers if these concepts might contribute to improvement of the project now or on the longterm? 
- Advice how to continu: Will there be an advice for future work or is there a good chance to create value by further investigating this for the group project.
- Update your planning if needed
  
This approach demonstrates you are able to assess new knowledge related to your problem domain, elaborate your decisions.

Note: Failure is ok, if the first conclusion is that something great can be done with the concepts and the conclusion shows it didn't. Know it  is also valuable information.


**core knowledge**

- Software Engineering methods & mindset (done)
- Discovering requirements that fit your environment.
- Relevant software architecture knowledge for complex contexts.
- Software Quality Characteristics,  with at least basic knowledge on scalability and security.
- Maintainable software & deployment environment.
