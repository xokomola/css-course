## What

The primary goal of applied research is to create professional products that meet the needs of the working field. The focus is on practical challenges that are context-specific, and the usage of a structured and methodical approach. In other words, as a software engineer you will be creating products like user stories, architecture documents, test reports and code, with methods like stakeholder analysis, (UML) decomposition, and unit testing.

## Why

Applied research bridges the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical application, solving specific problems and challenges for organizations. By focusing on professional products, such as prototypes improved processes, etc., applied research enhances professional awareness, cultivates critical thinking, and contributes to societal progress. Besides this, you learn skills that you can use a lifetime, such as problem-solving and adaptability.

## How

The research that you will be doing during this semester is integrated into the education during this semester. We expect you to execute research during all phases of a project (analysis, design, realisation), leading to professional IT products. The focus should be on doing actual research as part of your day-to-day activities as a junior IT professional. Consider the Technology Readiness Level (TRL) of your project while deciding upon the research you'll do and the methods you'll use. During the semester, you report on your research both verbally and in writing. 

As a group, you perform research to acquire basic knowledge on new concepts and demonstrate you can translate this to the context of the group project. This research can lead to direct realizable results (improvement of existing work) or advice on potential future improvements for the project. 

Besides the group project, we expect you to conduct individual or dual experiments, in which you focus on the personal flavour concepts, to work out your knowledge in working prototypes. Preferably these experiments result in insights that potentially contribute to the group project goals, although it is also possible to perform these experiments in a challenge of personal interest.
